Your phone is your PC’s trackpad and keyboard.

Telepad turns an Android phone into a trackpad, keyboard and media remote for Windows, Linux and macOS, over Wi‑Fi or Bluetooth. End-to-end encrypted. No account, no cloud.

How it works

Set up in about a minute.

No account to create and no cloud in between. Your phone talks straight to your PC.

  1. 1

    Install Telepad on your PC

    Run the installer (or open the app on a Mac). Telepad sits in your tray and shows a QR code. It can start with your computer, so it is always there.

  2. 2

    Scan the code with your phone

    Open the app and tap Scan QR code. The code holds your PC’s key, so there is nothing to type and nothing to compare.

  3. 3

    That’s it

    You are paired, and from then on the phone just connects. No account, no password, and what you do stays on your network.

Features

Everything you would want from a laptop trackpad.

And a few things you can only do with a phone.

A trackpad that behaves like one

Tap to click, two fingers to scroll, tap and drag to select, press and hold for a right click, three fingers for a middle click. The pad shows what it understood, so you learn it by using it.

  • Momentum scrolling, plus a scroll strip for one finger
  • Real mouse buttons that you can hold while you drag
  • Pointer speed and four acceleration curves, with a pad in the settings to try them on

A keyboard that speaks your PC’s language

Type with your phone’s own keyboard, autocorrect and all. Telepad works out the exact keystrokes that give your PC the same text. For everything a phone keyboard lacks, there is a row of real keys.

  • A full keyboard for keybinds: hold Super with one finger and tap with another, or tap it once for the next key, twice to lock
  • Shortcuts with the right keys for Windows, macOS or Linux
  • Copy text from the phone to the PC and back, only when you press the button

Media, slides and PC actions

Play, pause and skip. Turn the volume up or down, and hold the button to keep going. Use the phone as a slide clicker, or jump straight to show desktop, task view, a screenshot or the lock screen.

  • Slide controls with start, black screen and end
  • Actions named for your system: Task view, Mission Control or Overview
  • Uses your system’s media keys, so it works with most players

Wi‑Fi or Bluetooth

Wi‑Fi gives you everything, including the clipboard. Bluetooth makes your phone a standard keyboard and mouse, so the PC needs no app at all.

Reconnects by itself

If the Wi‑Fi hiccups, Telepad notices and reconnects quietly, and tells you why if it can’t. You can also keep it connected in the background.

Looks like it belongs

Material 3, light and dark, in an accent you choose or your wallpaper’s colors. Built for phones, tablets and landscape.

Made for everyone

Works with TalkBack, large text and reduced motion. Text and icon colors are tested against WCAG contrast.

Security

Private by design. Verified, not just trusted.

What you type controls your computer, so Telepad treats it that way.

  • Encrypted end to end

    The Noise protocol with X25519 and ChaCha20-Poly1305. Nothing on your network can read your typing, change it or play it back.

  • Scanning proves it is your PC

    The QR code on your PC’s screen carries the PC’s key, so a fake PC cannot take its place. (Without a camera, compare the 80-bit fingerprint on both screens instead.) If a PC ever answers with a different key, the app warns you instead of connecting.

  • Your PC decides who can pair

    A phone can control the PC only after it scans a code your PC shows. The code works for one phone, once, for five minutes.

  • What you type stays on your network

    No account, no cloud, no analytics, no ads. Your input goes to your PC and nowhere else. The only thing Telepad asks the internet is whether a newer version exists.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Do I need to install something on my PC?

For Wi‑Fi mode, yes: Telepad for your computer, a small app that sits in the tray (the menu bar on a Mac) and can start with your computer. Bluetooth mode needs nothing on the PC: your phone shows up as a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse.

Does it work away from home?

Telepad is made for a trusted local network: your phone and your PC on the same Wi‑Fi, or paired over Bluetooth. It is not meant to be reachable from the internet, and you should not forward its port.

What happens to what I type?

It goes straight to your PC, encrypted. There is no server in between, and nothing is sent to the developers or anyone else. The app has no analytics, ads or accounts. The one thing it asks the internet is GitHub’s public list of releases, once a day, to tell you when a newer version exists; that request carries no identifier, and you can switch it off.

Is it safe on a network I don’t control?

Once a phone is paired, other devices on the network can’t type on your PC, and they can’t read what you send. Do your first pairing on a network you trust, because that is the moment your PC lets a new phone in. Some public networks stop devices from seeing each other. Bluetooth works there.

Which systems does it support?

The phone must run Android 9 or later. The PC can run Windows 10 or 11, Linux (X11 and Wayland, through the kernel’s uinput) or macOS 11 and later. The Windows and macOS versions are built and tested on every change, but have had less hands-on testing than Linux, so please tell us if anything is odd.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not at the moment. Telepad is Android only.

Is it on Google Play or F-Droid?

Not yet. Download the signed APK from the GitHub releases. Every file is listed with its checksum, and has a build attestation that shows it was built from this repository.

How can I help?

Try it and tell us what you find. Bug reports, ideas, translations and patches are all welcome, and the contributing guide explains how to build and test both halves.
